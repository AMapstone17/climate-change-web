Greenhouse gases

Greenhouse gas concentrations, sea level rise, ocean heat and ocean acidification all reached record highs in 2021.

Climate change harms human health through air pollution, disease, extreme weather events, forced displacement, food
insecurity and pressures on mental health.

More than 8.7 million people currently die very year due to outdoor air pollution.

Burning natural gas was responsible for 22% of global carbon emissions from fuel combustion in 2020 (not far behind oil,
32%, and coal, 45%).

Burning fossil fuels creates a blanket of pollution trapping the sun's heat on Earth and raising global temperatures.

Global warming leads to changes like droughts, water scarcity, severe fires, rising sea levels, flooding, melting polar
ice, intense storms and declining biodiversity.

Today, the concentration of CO2 in the atmosphere is about 50% higher than in 1750, far exceeding the natural changes
over at least the past 800,000 years.

With every increment of global warming, extreme heat and rainfall events become more frequent and more intense.

Greenhouse gases from human activities are responsible for approximately 1.1°C of warming since 1850-1900

- This has already caused significant changes in the climate, including more extreme weather events, which have caused
  widespread harm to people and nature.
